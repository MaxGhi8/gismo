\section{Preliminaries} \label{sec:preliminaries}

In this section we fix the notation and recall the principal concepts needed in the
remainder of the paper. We first define B-splines and the associated
isogeometric spaces (Section~\ref{subsec:bsplines}), state the model problem and
its multipatch discretization (Section~\ref{subsec:model}), and summarize the
dual-primal Isogeometric Tearing and Interconnecting (IETI-DP) method
(Section~\ref{subsec:ieti}) \cite{kleiss2012ieti}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{B-splines and isogeometric spaces} \label{subsec:bsplines}

Given integers $m, p > 0$, a knot vector is a non-decreasing sequence
$\Xi := \{0 = \xi_1 \le \dots \le \xi_{m+p+1} = 1\}$, which we take \emph{open},
i.e.\ $\xi_1 = \dots = \xi_{p+1} = 0$ and $\xi_{m+1} = \dots = \xi_{m+p+1} = 1$.
The Cox--de Boor recursion~\cite{de1978practical} defines the univariate B-spline
basis functions $\bsuni{i}{p} : [0,1] \to \R$, $i = 1, \dots, m$:
\begin{equation*}
    \bsuni{i}{0}(\eta) =
    \begin{cases}
        1 & \text{if } \xi_i \le \eta < \xi_{i+1}, \\
        0 & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    \qquad
    \bsuni{i}{p}(\eta) =
    \frac{\eta - \xi_i}{\xi_{i+p} - \xi_i}\, \bsuni{i}{p-1}(\eta)
    + \frac{\xi_{i+p+1} - \eta}{\xi_{i+p+1} - \xi_{i+1}}\, \bsuni{i+1}{p-1}(\eta),
\end{equation*}
with the convention $0/0 = 0$; an interior knot of multiplicity $r$ yields
$C^{p-r}$ continuity~\cite{cottrell2009isogeometric}. The constructed basis functions span the univariate
spline space $\Shat{p} := \spann\{\bsuni{i}{p}\}_{i=1}^{m}$.
Multivariate B-splines are constructed with tensor products; on $\refdom := (0,1)^d$,
with a degree $p_l$ and an open knot vector per direction $l = 1, \dots, d$, we
set $\Bref{\vect{i}}{\vect{p}}(\vect{\eta}) := \prod_{l=1}^{d}
    \bsuni{i_l}{p_l}(\eta_l)$ for $\vect{\eta} \in \refdom$. Defining a
colexicographical reordering into a single index $i = 1, \dots, n$, with
$n := \prod_{l=1}^{d} m_l$, gives the reference function space $\Shat{\vect{p}} :=
    \spann\{\Bref{i}{\vect{p}}\}_{i=1}^{n}$.

The physical domain $\Omega \subset \R^{D}$ ($D \ge d$) is the image
$\Omega = \gmap(\refdom)$ of a \emph{geometry map}
$\gmap = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \vect{P}_i\, \Bref{i}{\vect{p}} \in
    [\Shat{\vect{p}}]^{D}$ with control points $\vect{P}_i \in \R^{D}$.
We assume that the geometry map is invertible with full-rank Jacobian $J_{\gmap} = D\gmap$.
For $D = d$ the domain is planar or volumetric; for $D > d$ it is a manifold (e.g.\ a
surface in $\R^3$), on which physical gradients use the Moore--Penrose
pseudo-inverse $J_{\gmap}^{\dagger} = (J_{\gmap}^{\top} J_{\gmap})^{-1}
    J_{\gmap}^{\top}$ of the (full-column-rank, non-square) Jacobian, namely
$\nabla_\Omega u=(J_{\gmap}^{\dagger})^\top\nabla_{\vect\eta}\widehat u$.
This is the left inverse appropriate to the tall matrix
$J_{\gmap}\in\R^{D\times d}$, $D>d$
(the right inverse $J_{\gmap}^{\top}(J_{\gmap}J_{\gmap}^{\top})^{-1}$ is undefined
here, since $J_{\gmap}J_{\gmap}^{\top}\in\R^{D\times D}$ has rank $d<D$) \mg{Check this}.
By the isoparametric paradigm~\cite{hughes2005isogeometric}, the isogeometric
space on $\Omega$ is
\begin{equation}
    \Vglo := \spann\bigl\{ \Bphy{i}{\vect{p}} := \Bref{i}{\vect{p}} \circ
    \gmap^{-1} \ \big|\ i = 1, \dots, n \bigr\}.
    \label{eq:isogeometric-space}
\end{equation}
NURBS are obtained by assigning weights $w_i > 0$ and replacing
$\Bref{i}{\vect{p}}$ with $\Rnurbs{i}{\vect{p}} = w_i
    \Bref{i}{\vect{p}} / \sum_{j=1}^n w_j \Bref{j}{\vect{p}}$ (B-splines
being the case $w_i \equiv 1$); all that follows is unchanged.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Model problem and multipatch discretization} \label{subsec:model}

As a model problem we take the scalar diffusion equation on an open, bounded,
connected Lipschitz domain $\Omega \subset \R^{D}$ with $\partial\Omega =
    \overline{\Gamma_D} \cup \overline{\Gamma_N}$, $\Gamma_D \cap \Gamma_N =
    \emptyset$. Given $\alpha \in L^\infty(\Omega)$ with $\alpha \ge \alpha_0 > 0$,
$f \in L^2(\Omega)$ and boundary data $g_D, g_N$, we seek $u$ with
\begin{equation}
    -\nabla \cdot (\alpha \nabla u) = f \ \text{ in } \Omega,
    \qquad u = g_D \ \text{ on } \Gamma_D,
    \qquad \partial_n u = g_N \ \text{ on } \Gamma_N.
    \label{eq:poisson-strong}
\end{equation}
The weak form reads: find $u \in V_{g_D} := \{ v \in H^1(\Omega) : v_{|\Gamma_D}
    = g_D \}$ such that
\begin{equation}
    a(u, v) = F(v) \qquad \forall\, v \in V_0 := \{ v \in H^1(\Omega) :
    v_{|\Gamma_D} = 0 \},
    \label{eq:weak-form}
\end{equation}
with $a(u, v) := \int_{\Omega} \alpha\, \nabla u \cdot \nabla v\,\mathrm{d}\Omega$
and $F(v) := \int_{\Omega} f v \,\mathrm{d}\Omega + \int_{\Gamma_N} g_N v
    \,\mathrm{d}s$. If $\Gamma_D = \emptyset$ (a closed surface, or pure Neumann
data), $a(\cdot,\cdot)$ is only positive semidefinite, the problem is then solvable only under the compatibility condition
$\int_\Omega f \,\mathrm{d}\Omega + \int_{\partial\Omega} g_N \,\mathrm{d}s = 0$,
and its solution is unique up to a constant, for example by fixing the zero mean condition $\int_\Omega u
    \,\mathrm{d}\Omega = 0$.

We discretize on a non-overlapping multipatch decomposition into $\npatch$
patches,
\begin{equation}
    \overline{\Omega} = \bigcup_{k=1}^{\npatch} \overline{\patchdom{k}},
    \qquad \patchdom{k} \cap \patchdom{l} = \emptyset \ \text{ for } k,l = 1, \ldots, \npatch, \text{ and } k \neq l,
    \label{eq:multipatch}
\end{equation}
each the image $\patchdom{k} = \gmapk{k}(\refdom)$, $k = 1, \ldots, \npatch$, of its own geometry
map, carrying a local isogeometric space $\Vloc{k}$ of dimension $n_k$ as
in~\eqref{eq:isogeometric-space}. The patches are assumed \emph{fully
    matching}~\cite{kleiss2012ieti}, adjacent patches share a complete edge/face with
coinciding basis traces, so that the conforming global space $\Vglo :=
    \{ v \in H^1(\Omega) : v_{|\patchdom{k}} \in \Vloc{k}, \ k=1, \ldots, \npatch \}$ is well defined. Given a
basis $\{\Bphy{i}{\vect{p}}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ of $\Vglo$, after fixing the Dirichlet boundary conditions we define $N := \dim \Vglo$, and the
Galerkin discretization of~\eqref{eq:weak-form} is the following linear system
\begin{equation}
    \Aglo\, \uglo = \fglo, \qquad
    \Aglo_{ij} = a(\Bphy{j}{\vect{p}}, \Bphy{i}{\vect{p}}), \quad
    \fglo_i = F(\Bphy{i}{\vect{p}}), \quad i, j = 1, \dots, N.
    \label{eq:global-system}
\end{equation}
The matrix $\Aglo$ is symmetric positive definite (SPD) when $\Gamma_D \neq \emptyset$, but $\Aglo$ becomes
increasingly ill-conditioned under mesh refinement and
degree elevation~\cite{gahalaut2019condition, sangalli2016isogeometric},
then suitable preconditioners for Krylov
methods need to be design~\cite{van2003iterative}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The IETI-DP method} \label{subsec:ieti}

Dual-primal Isogeometric Tearing and Interconnecting (IETI-DP) is the
isogeometric counterpart of the FETI-DP family of non-overlapping domain
decomposition methods~\cite{farhat1991method, toselli2004domain}.
The patches of~\eqref{eq:multipatch} serve directly as subdomains, so no
auxiliary mesh partitioning is required.
A defining feature of IETI-DP is that \emph{all dominant computational
    work is patch-local and parallelizable}: local stiffness assembly,
Cholesky factorization of local augmented systems, and the per-iteration
back-solves in the scaled Dirichlet preconditioner are each fully independent
across patches \cite{kleiss2012ieti}. Only the coarse problem, of size $\nprim \times \nprim$ with $\nprim = O(K)$
primal dofs, requires global communication.

\paragraph{Local problems and jump operator.}
The discretization for IETI-DP method is define over the non-continuous
space $\Vprod := \prod_{k=1}^{\npatch}
    \Vloc{k} \supset \Vglo$. Each patch carries the local stiffness matrix and load vector
\begin{equation}
    (\Aloc{k})_{ij} := \int_{\patchdom{k}} \alpha\, \nabla \Bloc{k}{j} \cdot
    \nabla \Bloc{k}{i} \,\mathrm{d}\Omega, \qquad
    (\floc{k})_{i} := \int_{\patchdom{k}} f \Bloc{k}{i} \,\mathrm{d}\Omega
    + \int_{\Gamma_N \cap \partial\patchdom{k}} g_N \Bloc{k}{i} \,\mathrm{d}s,
    \label{eq:local-stiffness}
\end{equation}
with $i, j = 1, \dots, n_k$ and $k=1, \ldots, \npatch$, where $\{\Bloc{k}{i}\}_{i=1}^{n_k}$ is the basis of
$\Vloc{k}$. Thus $\Aloc{k} \in \R^{n_k \times n_k}$ are SPD matrices
and $\uloc{k} \in \R^{n_k}$ represents $u_k = \sum_i (\uloc{k})_i \Bloc{k}{i}$.
Continuity across patch interfaces is enforced via Lagrange multipliers
$\lagmult \in \R^{\Lambda}$. Introducing the signed Boolean \emph{jump matrices}
$\Jloc{k} \in \R^{\Lambda \times n_k}$ and $\Jfull := (\Jloc{1}, \dots, \Jloc{\npatch})$,
the continuity constraint can be written as $\sum_{k=1}^{\npatch} \Jloc{k} \uloc{k} = \vect{0}$.
Minimizing the broken energy $\sum_k (\tfrac12 \uloc{k}^{\top} \Aloc{k} \uloc{k} -
    \floc{k}^{\top} \uloc{k})$ subject to the continuity constraint, with
multiplier $\lagmult \in \R^{\Lambda}$, gives the saddle-point system
\begin{equation}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \Aloc{1} &        &                & \Jloc{1}^{\top}       \\
                 & \ddots &                & \vdots                \\
                 &        & \Aloc{\npatch} & \Jloc{\npatch}^{\top} \\
        \Jloc{1} & \cdots & \Jloc{\npatch} & 0
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \uloc{1} \\ \vdots \\ \uloc{\npatch} \\ \lagmult
    \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \floc{1} \\ \vdots \\ \floc{\npatch} \\ \vect{0}
    \end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:ieti-saddle}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Primal constraints and Schur complement.}
The blocks $\Aloc{k}$ of floating patches ($\partial\patchdom{k} \cap \Gamma_D =
    \emptyset$) are singular. The dual-primal approach remove the singularities selecting some \emph{primal}
degrees of freedom (dof), usually corner values and/or interface averages, stacked as the
rows of a constraint matrix $\Cloc{k} \in \R^{q_k \times n_k}$.
This regularizes the local blocks and introduces a global
coarse subdomain $k = \npatch + 1$:
\begin{equation}
    \Aaug{k} =
    \begin{pmatrix} \Aloc{k} & \Cloc{k}^{\top} \\ \Cloc{k} & 0 \end{pmatrix},
    \quad
    \Jaug{k} = \begin{pmatrix} \Jloc{k} & 0 \end{pmatrix},
    \quad
    \faug{k} = \begin{pmatrix} \floc{k} \\ \vect{0} \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \begin{aligned}
        \Aaug{\npatch+1} & = \textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\npatch} \Psik{k} \Aloc{k} \Psik{k}^{\top}, \\
        \Jaug{\npatch+1} & = \textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\npatch} \Jloc{k} \Psik{k}^{\top},          \\
        \faug{\npatch+1} & = \textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\npatch} \Psik{k} \floc{k}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:augmented-blocks}
\end{equation}
With $\nprim$ we indicate the number of primal degrees of freedom, and $\Psik{k} \in
    \R^{\nprim \times n_k}$ represents the energy-minimizing primal basis functions;
see~\cite{kleiss2012ieti} for the explicit construction. Eliminating the
$\uaug{k}$ leaves an SPD system for the multipliers alone,
\begin{equation}
    \Fschur \lagmult = \gschur,
    \quad
    \Fschur = \sum_{k=1}^{\npatch+1} \Jaug{k} \Aaug{k}^{-1}
    \Jaug{k}^{\top},
    \quad
    \gschur = \sum_{k=1}^{\npatch+1} \Jaug{k} \Aaug{k}^{-1}
    \faug{k}.
    \label{eq:ieti-schur}
\end{equation}
The system is solved by conjugate gradient (CG), and $\uglo$ is
recovered from $\lagmult$ solving the coarse global problem \eqref{eq:augmented-blocks}
and the addressing the local problems in parallel.

\paragraph{Scaled Dirichlet preconditioner.}
The system~\eqref{eq:ieti-schur} is large and ill-conditioned, so CG is
preconditioned by the scaled Dirichlet preconditioner~\cite{kleiss2012ieti,
    pechstein2013finite}
\begin{equation}
    \Psd = \sum_{k=1}^{\npatch}
    \Jskel{k}\, \Dmult{k}^{-1}\, \Sloc{k}\, \Dmult{k}^{-1}\, \Jskel{k}^{\top}.
    \label{eq:scaled-dirichlet}
\end{equation}
Here $\Jskel{k}$ is the restriction of $\Jloc{k}$ to the skeleton dofs $\skel_k$
(interfaces and Neumann boundary, complementary to the interior $I_k$).
The diagonal matrix $\Dmult{k}$ is the multiplicity scaling, with
$(\Dmult{k})_{jj} = 1/\mathrm{mult}(j)$ where $\mathrm{mult}(j)$ counts the
number of patches sharing dof $j$.
Finally, $\Sloc{k}$ is the local Dirichlet Schur complement of $\Aloc{k}$ onto
the skeleton. Partitioning $\Aloc{k}$ into interior and skeleton blocks,
\begin{equation}
    \Sloc{k} = A^{(k)}_{\skel\skel}
    - A^{(k)}_{\skel I}\, \bigl(A^{(k)}_{II}\bigr)^{-1}\, A^{(k)}_{I\skel}
    \ \in\ \R^{|\skel_k| \times |\skel_k|}.
    \label{eq:local-schur}
\end{equation}
The overall method is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:ieti-dp}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{IETI-DP solver for the model problem~\eqref{eq:poisson-strong}.}
    \label{alg:ieti-dp}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require multipatch $\{\patchdom{k}\}_{k=1}^{\npatch}$, data $(\alpha, f, g_D, g_N)$,
        choice of primal degrees of freedom
        \Ensure  discrete continuous approximated solution $u_h \in \Vglo$
        \vspace{0.2cm}
        \Statex \textbf{Assembly and setup}
        \ParFor{k = 1, \dots, \npatch} \Comment{parallel over patches}
        \State assemble local stiffness $\Aloc{k}$ and load $\floc{k}$
        \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:local-stiffness}}
        \State form jump matrix $\Jloc{k}$ and primal constraints $\Cloc{k}$
        \State build augmented blocks $\Aaug{k}, \Jaug{k},
            \faug{k}$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:augmented-blocks}}
        \State factorize $\Aaug{k}$ \Comment{one Cholesky per patch; used in all CG steps}
        \EndParFor
        \State assemble and factorize coarse blocks $\Aaug{\npatch+1},
            \Jaug{\npatch+1}, \faug{\npatch+1}$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:augmented-blocks}}
        \vspace{0.2cm}
        \Statex \textbf{Interface dual problem}
        \State $\gschur \gets \sum_{k=1}^{\npatch+1} \Jaug{k}
            \Aaug{k}^{-1} \faug{k}$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:ieti-schur}}
        \State solve $\Fschur \lagmult = \gschur$ by preconditioned CG, where $\Fschur = \sum_{k=1}^{\npatch+1} \Jaug{k} \Aaug{k}^{-1} \Jaug{k}^{\top}$
        \vspace{0.2cm}
        \Statex \textbf{Reconstruction}
        \State solve the primal problem:
        $\uaug{\npatch+1} \gets \Aaug{\npatch+1}^{-1}
            (\faug{\npatch+1} - \Jaug{\npatch+1}^{\top} \lagmult)$ \Comment{global coarse problem}
        \ParFor{k = 1, \dots, \npatch} \Comment{parallel over patches}
        \State $\uaug{k} \gets \Aaug{k}^{-1}
            (\faug{k} - \Jaug{k}^{\top} \lagmult)$
        \EndParFor
        \State assemble $u_h \in \Vglo$ from $\{\uaug{k}\}_{k=1}^{\npatch}$
        \State \Return $u_h$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{rmk}[Parallel structure and serial cost] \label{rmk:schur-cost}
    Each preconditioned CG step applies $\Psd$ via $K$ local Schur-complement
    solves (Eq.~\eqref{eq:local-schur}), each requiring one interior back-solve
    with the precomputed factorization of $\Aaug{k}$.
    These $K$ solves are mutually independent and constitute the dominant
    per-iteration cost~\cite[\S4.5]{kleiss2012ieti}.
    On a single processor: $O(K p\,n_k^{3/2})$ per CG step.
    The ideal case is to have $K$ processors in order to have the
    total cost independet from $K$, i.e. $O(p\,n_k^{3/2})$ per CG step.
\end{rmk}

\section{The Neural Schur Preconditioner}
\label{sec:neural-prec}

The per-iteration cost of IETI-DP is dominated by evaluating $\Sloc{k}$ for
every patch in the scaled Dirichlet preconditioner~\eqref{eq:scaled-dirichlet}
(Remark~\ref{rmk:schur-cost}).
We propose to replace each such solve with one evaluation of a learned linear
operator $\Snn(\,\cdot\,;\vect{\zeta}_k)$ conditioned on the patch geometry and
shared across all patches.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Formulation}
\label{subsec:formulation}

Let $\mathcal F_k$ be the indices left after strong elimination of Dirichlet
degrees of freedom and let $n_k^f=|\mathcal F_k|$.  The \emph{geometry
    descriptor} $\vect{\zeta}_k \in \R^{n_k^f \times (D+1)}$ of patch $k$ collects,
for every $i\in\mathcal F_k$, the physical coordinates of the Greville
abscissa $\gmapk{k}(\vect{\eta}^{\,\mathrm{Gr}}_i) \in \R^D$ \cite{cottrell2009isogeometric}, and its NURBS weight $w_i$,
\begin{equation}
    \vect{\zeta}_k
    = \bigl\{ \bigl(\gmapk{k}(\vect{\eta}^{\,\mathrm{Gr}}_i),\, w_i\bigr)
    \bigr\}_{i\in\mathcal F_k}.
    \label{eq:geom-features}
\end{equation}
We define the \emph{neural Schur operator} with shared weights $\theta$ as
\begin{equation}
    \Snn\bigl(\,\cdot\,;\, \vect{\zeta}_k\bigr)
    : \R^{|\skel_k|} \to \R^{|\skel_k|},
    \qquad
    \vect{v} \mapsto \Snn(\vect{v}; \vect{\zeta}_k) \approx \Sloc{k} \vect{v},
    \label{eq:neural-schur}
\end{equation}
and the \emph{neural scaled Dirichlet preconditioner} by substituting \eqref{eq:neural-schur}
into \eqref{eq:scaled-dirichlet}:
\begin{equation}
    \Pneural = \sum_{k=1}^{\npatch}
    \Jskel{k}\, \Dmult{k}^{-1}\, \Snn(\,\cdot\,;\vect{\zeta}_k)\,
    \Dmult{k}^{-1}\, \Jskel{k}^{\top}.
    \label{eq:neural-prec}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{subsec:architecture}

The Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) model has fixed capacities
$N_{\max}$ geometry tokens and
$S_{\max}$ skeleton entries.  Let $E_k$ denote zero-padding from $n_k^f$ to
$N_{\max}$ tokens and let $R_k\in\R^{|\skel_k|\times S_{\max}}$ restrict a
padded skeleton vector to its active entries.  The network takes only
$\widehat{\vect{\zeta}}_k=E_k\vect{\zeta}_k$ as input and defines
$M_{\theta,k}\in\R^{|\skel_k|\times|\skel_k|}$, so that
\begin{equation}
    \Snn(\vect{v};\,\vect{\zeta}_k) = M_{\theta,k}\vect{v}.
    \label{eq:matrix-form}
\end{equation}
Linearity in $\vect{v}$ is immediate since $\vect{v}$ is not an input to the
network.
$M_{\theta,k}$ is parameterized in the SPD factorized form
\begin{equation}
    M_{\theta,k}
    =R_k\left(
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{\dv}}\,
    Q_\theta(\widehat{\vect{\zeta}}_k)\,
    Q_\theta(\widehat{\vect{\zeta}}_k)^{\top}
    + \varepsilon_\theta I_{S_{\max}}\right)R_k^\top,
    \label{eq:spd-param}
\end{equation}
where $Q_\theta(\widehat{\vect{\zeta}}_k)\in
    \R^{S_{\max}\times\dv}$, $\dv$ is the latent dimension, and
$\varepsilon_\theta=\operatorname{softplus}(\widehat\varepsilon_\theta)>0$
is a learned scalar.  Therefore $M_{\theta,k}$ is SPD for every network
output.  Its matrix-free application costs $O(S_{\max}\dv)$ through two
dense matrix--vector products; the Transformer is evaluated only once per
patch during setup and the factors are then cached.

The factor $Q_\theta(\widehat{\vect{\zeta}}_k)$ is computed by the IgaNet
Transformer encoder.  The padded descriptor is treated as a sequence of
$N_{\max}$ tokens in $\R^{D+1}$, processed through a learnable affine
embedding plus sinusoidal positional encoding,
followed by $L$ layers of multi-head self-attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention}.
A cross-attention resampling step then maps the encoded sequence to
$G_\theta(\widehat{\vect{\zeta}}_k)\in\R^{S_{\max}\times\dv}$ using
$S_{\max}$ learned target queries, and a final linear projection gives
$Q_\theta=G_\theta W_Q$.  In the present implementation
$(N_{\max},S_{\max})=(304,48)$.  Zero padding is part of the tensor contract.
The original Yeti-only checkpoint supplies no padding mask, so a patch far
below these capacities must be treated as out of distribution.  The
geometry-diverse model in Section~\ref{subsec:nn-diverse-data} corrects this
with an explicit mask.

\begin{rmk}[PCG compatibility]
    \label{rmk:linearity}
    Each $M_{\theta,k}$ is fixed during a solve and SPD.  Hence
    $\Pneural$ is symmetric positive semidefinite and is positive definite on the
    admissible multiplier space when the assembled scaled jump operators have full
    row rank (after redundant multiplier constraints are removed), exactly the
    space on which~\eqref{eq:ieti-schur} is SPD.  Thus standard preconditioned
    conjugate gradient (PCG) applies; a
    flexible Krylov method would only be necessary if the learned factors were
    updated during the iteration~\cite{saad1993flexible}.
\end{rmk}

\subsection{Training}
\label{subsec:training}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
The generator produces $N_{\mathrm{data}}=20\,000$ rows in total,
distributed as evenly as possible over the 21 yeti patches.  For each row a
probe vector $\vect{v}^{(i)} \in \R^{|\skel_k|}$ is drawn from a Gaussian
random field (GRF) on the skeleton~\cite{adler2010geometry} with exponential
covariance
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Cov}\bigl(\vect{v}(\vect{x}),\, \vect{v}(\vect{y})\bigr)
    = \sigma_c^2 \exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\|\vect{x}-\vect{y}\|}{\ell_c}\Bigr),
    \qquad \ell_c = 0.005,\quad \sigma_c = 1,
    \label{eq:grf-cov}
\end{equation}
following~\cite{kopanivcakova2025deeponet}.
Here $\vect{x},\vect{y}\in\R^D$ are physical skeleton coordinates,
$\ell_c$ is the physical correlation length, and $\sigma_c$ is the marginal
standard deviation.  The subscript $c$ anticipates that this is the dataset for
the coarse training discretization introduced below.
The probe vectors are not network inputs; they evaluate the loss.
Targets $\Sloc{k}\vect{v}^{(i)}$ are computed from the local factorizations in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ieti-dp}.
Rows are shuffled and split into $16\,000/2\,000/2\,000$ training,
validation, and test samples.  Since every patch contributes rows before this
split, the test loss measures new probes on the same 21 geometries; it is not a
held-out-geometry generalization estimate.  The teapot experiment in
Section~\ref{sec:nn-comparison} supplies that stricter test.

\paragraph{Loss.}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{train}}
    = \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{train}}}
    \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{train}}}
    \bigl\|M_{\theta,k(i)}\,\vect{v}^{(i)}
    - \Sloc{k(i)}\vect{v}^{(i)}
    \bigr\|_2.
    \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Here $N_{\mathrm{train}}=16\,000$ and $k(i)\in\{1,\ldots,21\}$ identifies the
patch from which sample $i$ was generated.

\paragraph{Optimizer and hyperparameters.}
Training uses AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019decoupledweightdecayregularization}
with batch size 100.  The selected full-rank candidate uses $\dv=48$, four
attention heads, three geometry-encoder layers, learning rate $10^{-3}$,
weight decay $10^{-4}$ and zero dropout.  It is trained in successive
100-epoch stages, reloading the weights and restarting the optimizer between
stages.  The lower-rank reference has $\dv=20$.  The accompanying
\texttt{ray\_tune.py} can run TPE~\cite{watanabe2023tree,bergstra2015hyperopt}
and ASHA~\cite{li2020massivelyparallelhyperparametertuning} through
HyperNOs~\cite{ghiotto2025hypernos}; this facility was not used to select the
reported checkpoint, so no hyperparameter-search claim is made here.  Its
configured search space is:

\section{Reference benchmark}
\label{sec:reference-benchmark}

All experiments run on an AMD Ryzen 5 7600 processor (six physical cores,
12 hardware threads) with shared-memory parallelism, stopping tolerance
$10^{-8}$, and maximum $1000$ iterations.
Timings are the mean over five repetitions.

\subsection{Setup} \label{subsec:bench-setup}

We compare Direct Cholesky; GMRES and CG with no preconditioner, Jacobi, and symmetric Gauss--Seidel;
geometric multigrid as a standalone solver; and the IETI-DP solver of
Section~\ref{subsec:ieti} (with and without the scaled Dirichlet preconditioner).
The geometric multigrid uses Gauss--Seidel smoothing (one pre-/post-smooth step,
V-cycle; levels equal to the number of $h$-refinement steps)
Geometry, basis, boundary
conditions, and right-hand side are identical across all methods.
Two test geometries are considered: a planar B-spline multipatch domain in $\R^2$
(Section~\ref{subsec:bench-2d}) and an open NURBS surface in $\R^3$
(Section~\ref{subsec:bench-3d}).
Each sweep varies one of three parameters: the number of uniform patch splits
($\times 2^d$ subdomains per split), the local polynomial degree $p$, or the
uniform $h$-refinement level.

\subsection{Theoretical convergence rates}
\label{subsec:theory}

We collect the asymptotic estimates against which the parameter studies of
Sections~\ref{subsec:bench-2d}--\ref{subsec:bench-3d} are compared.
Throughout, $N$ is the number of coupled DOFs, $K$ the number of patches,
$n_k = N/K$ the average local patch size, and $p$ the spline degree.
On the fixed physical domain $\Omega$ ($d=2$ for both benchmarks) the patch
diameter is $H\sim K^{-1/2}$ and the \emph{global} mesh size is $h\sim N^{-1/2}$,
so the number of elements across a patch is $H/h\sim n_k^{1/2}$, independent
of $K$. We stress the distinction between the two sweeps that vary the
partition: \emph{refinement} fixes $K$ and grows $n_k$, whereas \emph{patch
    splitting} fixes $n_k$ and grows $K$ (with $N=K\,n_k$). Table~\ref{tab:theory} summarises the predicted serial and parallel
($K$ processors, one per patch) costs with explicit dependence on $n_k$, $K$,
and $p$; the text below justifies each entry.

\paragraph{Direct solver.}
For a 2-D elliptic problem on a quasi-uniform mesh, nested
dissection~\cite[Thm.~4.1]{george1973nested} produces a Cholesky factorization in
$O(N^{3/2}) = O(K^{3/2}n_k^{3/2})$ operations.
For IGA with tensor-product B-splines of degree $p$, each basis function
couples $O(p^d)$ neighbours; the nested-dissection separators grow by a
factor $p$, raising the factorization cost to $O(p^3 N^{3/2})$ in 2D.

\paragraph{Conjugate gradient.}
The stiffness matrix condition number satisfies~\cite[Thm.~4.3]{gahalaut2019condition}
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(A) \;\sim\; h^{-2} \;\sim\; N \;=\; K\,n_k .
    \label{eq:kappa-cg}
\end{equation}
Denoting the error at step $k$ by $e_k = x_*-x_k$ and the
energy norm by $\|v\|_A=(Av,v)^{1/2}$, the CG convergence
bound~\cite[Thm.~6.29]{saad2003iterative} gives
$\|e_k\|_{A} \le 2\!\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{\kappa(A)}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa(A)}+1}\right)^{\!k}\|e_0\|_{A}$,
the number of iterations to reach tolerance $\varepsilon$ scales as
$O\!\left(\sqrt{\kappa(A)}\log(1/\varepsilon)\right)\sim O\!\left(N^{1/2}\right)
    =O\!\left((K n_k)^{1/2}\right)$
and the total serial solve cost as
$O\!\left(N^{3/2}\right)=O\!\left(K^{3/2} n_k^{3/2}\right)$.
For degree elevation at fixed $h$ the condition number grows
super-algebraically: $\kappa(A)\leq C\,p^{2d+2}4^{pd}$~\cite[Thm.~4.8]{gahalaut2019condition},
making unpreconditioned CG rapidly intractable as $p$ increases.
\\
Jacobi and symmetric Gauss--Seidel preconditioners reduce the spectral radius but leave the asymptotic order
of the condition number unchanged:
$\kappa(M_{\mathrm{J}}^{-1}A) = O(h^{-2})$ and
$\kappa(M_{\mathrm{GS}}^{-1}A) = O(h^{-2})$.
The iteration count therefore retains the same
$O\!\left(N^{1/2}\right)=O\!\left((K n_k)^{1/2}\right)$
scaling with an improved (but not asymptotically better) constant.

\paragraph{GMRES.}
GMRES bounds the residual in the Euclidean $2$-norm $\|r_k\|_2$ ($r_k=b-Ax_k$),
not the error energy norm~\cite[Cor.~6.33]{saad2003iterative}. For the normal SPD
system of~\eqref{eq:poisson-strong} that bound reduces to
$\|r_k\|_2\le 2\bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt{\kappa(A)}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa(A)}+1}\bigr)^{k}\|r_0\|_2$,
the same Chebyshev factor as the CG error bound~\cite[Thm.~6.29]{saad2003iterative}.
Hence the same $O\!\left(N^{1/2}\right)=O\!\left((K n_k)^{1/2}\right)$ iteration
count, $O\!\left(N^{3/2}\right)=O\!\left(K^{3/2} n_k^{3/2}\right)$ total cost, and
condition-number and degree scaling as CG.

\paragraph{Multigrid.}
For an $H^{2}$-regular elliptic problem on a quasi-uniform mesh of a flat domain,
the symmetric $V$-cycle with one pre- and one post-smoothing step satisfies the
smoothing and approximation properties of the variational multigrid theory and
therefore admits a contraction factor $\rho<1$ bounded independently of the mesh
size $h$~\cite{trottenberg2000multigrid}.
Used as a preconditioner this yields $\kappa(M_{\mathrm{MG}}^{-1}A)=O(1)$, hence
$O(1)$ iterations and an optimal $O(N)$ total solve cost.
This $h$-robustness is, however, neither uniform in the degree $p$ nor uniform
under strong geometric distortion; both properties are observed in
Section~\ref{subsubsec:bench-3d-sweeps}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item[(i)] \textbf{Degree.}
          For tensor-product splines of degree $p$, the spectral (symbol) analysis of the
          Galerkin matrices shows that a fixed-size subspace of high-frequency,
          maximal-continuity ($C^{p-1}$) modes is increasingly poorly damped by classical
          point smoothers (Jacobi, Gauss--Seidel) as $p$ grows; consequently the
          contraction factor of the standard $V$-cycle deteriorates towards $1$ and the
          iteration count grows with $p$~\cite{donatelli2015robust,tielen2020pmultigrid}.
          % Restoring $p$-robustness requires spectrally corrected (multi-iterative)
          % smoothers~\cite{donatelli2015robust} or smoothers based on stable splittings of
          % the spline space, for which a contraction factor independent of both $h$ and $p$
          % has been proved~\cite{hofreither2017robust}; the standard point-smoothed
          % $V$-cycle used here is deliberately taken as the baseline.
    \item[(ii)] \textbf{Geometry.}
          On a curved manifold the diffusion bilinear form pulls back to an elliptic
          problem on $\refdom$ with the spatially varying, generally anisotropic
          coefficient tensor $\sqrt{\det G}\,G^{-1}$, where $G=J_{\gmap}^{\top}J_{\gmap}$ is
          the first fundamental form of the geometry map, see Eq.~\eqref{eq:laplace-beltrami}.
          Strong, geometry-induced anisotropy and variation of $G^{-1}$ violate the
          constant-coefficient heuristics behind isotropic $h$-coarsening with point
          smoothers: the smoothing property degrades, the contraction factor need not stay
          below $1$, and the iteration count may grow with $N$ or stagnate
          altogether~\cite{trottenberg2000multigrid}.
\end{itemize}


\paragraph{IETI-DP with scaled Dirichlet preconditioner.}
The spectral bound for the preconditioned interface
system~\eqref{eq:ieti-schur}--\eqref{eq:scaled-dirichlet}
\cite{schneckenleitner2020condition} is:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa\!\left(M_{sD}^{-1}F\right)
    \;\leq\; C\,p\,\bigl(1+\log p+\log(H/h)\bigr)^{2},
    \label{eq:kappa-ieti}
\end{equation}
where $C>0$ is independent of $h$, $H$, $K$, $N$, and $p$.
By the CG convergence bound~\cite[Thm.~6.29]{saad2003iterative}, the number of iterations to
reach tolerance $\varepsilon$ scales as
$O\!\left(\sqrt{\kappa(M_{sD}^{-1}F)}\,\log(1/\varepsilon)\right)$,
and the dominant per-iteration cost is $O(K\,p\,n_k^{3/2})$ in serial
and $O(p\,n_k^{3/2})$ on $K$ parallel processors (Remark~\ref{rmk:schur-cost}).
Three consequences govern the parameter sweeps.
\begin{itemize}
    \item[(i)] \textbf{Refinement} ($K$ fixed): $H/h\sim n_k^{1/2}$
          gives $\kappa=O(\log^{2}n_k)$ and hence $O\!\left(\sqrt{\kappa}\right)=O(\log n_k)$ CG iterations.
    \item[(ii)] \textbf{Subdomain} ($h,p$ fixed per patch): $H/h\sim n_k^{1/2}$
          stays constant as $K$ grows, so the bound~\eqref{eq:kappa-ieti} is
          independent of $K$ and predicts near-constant conditioning.
          % The mild increase of the IETI-DP iteration count observed
          % with $K$ (Sections~\ref{subsubsec:bench-2d-sweeps}--\ref{subsubsec:bench-3d-sweeps})
          % reflects the constant in~\eqref{eq:kappa-ieti} and the inexactness of the
          % coarse solve, not a missing power of $K$; the dominant $K$-growth of the cost
          % is algebraic, since each CG step performs $K$ local back-solves and the
          % Lagrange-multiplier system grows with $K$.
    \item[(iii)] \textbf{Degree} ($h$ fixed):
          $\kappa=O\!\left(p\,(1+\log p)^{2}\right)$ \cite{schneckenleitner2020condition},
          giving $O\!\left(\sqrt{p}\,(1+\log p)\right)$ CG iterations.
          % , this is algebraic growth
          % that is far milder than the super-algebraic $\kappa(A_h)\leq C\,p^{2d+2}4^{pd}$
          % for unpreconditioned CG.
\end{itemize}

After solving the system~\eqref{eq:ieti-schur}, the reconstruction step (Algorithm~\ref{alg:ieti-dp}, lines 10--15)
recovers each $\uaug{k}$ by one back-solve with the precomputed $\Aaug{k}$
factorization, costing $O(K\,p\,n_k^{3/2})$ in serial or $O(p\,n_k^{3/2})$
in parallel. This is equivalent to a single CG iteration, so the total solve cost is driven by
the cost of the resolution of the interface problem~\eqref{eq:ieti-schur}.

\emph{(ii)~Polynomial degree} (Figures~\ref{fig:yeti-time-p},

point-smoothed multigrid (Section~\ref{subsec:theory}): multigrid (standalone)
iterations increase from $20$ to $488$ (a $24\times$ growth, consistent with the
contraction factor approaching $1$ as $p$
grows~\cite{donatelli2015robust,tielen2020pmultigrid}), while IETI-DP iterations
grow from $13$ to only $18$---roughly one additional iteration per unit increase
in $p$, far milder than the $O(p^{3/2})$ that the $O(p^{3})$ bound on $C$
in~\eqref{eq:kappa-ieti} would imply~\cite{kleiss2012ieti}.
Note that at $p=6$, CG (no preconditioner) also fails to converge within $1000$
iterations on this flat domain.

\emph{(iii)~Number of subdomains} (Figures~\ref{fig:yeti-time-K},
\ref{fig:yeti-iters-K}):
IETI-DP leads for $K\leq 336$ ($10.8\times$ at $K=21$, $1.7\times$ at $K=336$)
and falls behind at $K=1344$ (multigrid $1.7\times$ faster) and $K=5376$
(multigrid $3.9\times$ faster, $0.98\,$s vs.\ $3.85\,$s).
Multigrid (standalone) iteration counts remain essentially constant at $16$--$27$
across all split levels, confirming $K$-independence of the multigrid condition
number on this flat geometry.
The unpreconditioned (and Jacobi/Gauss--Seidel) Krylov solvers instead show
iteration counts that grow with $K$ (Figure~\ref{fig:yeti-iters-K}), consistent
with the corrected estimate $\kappa(A)\sim K$ and the resulting $O(\sqrt{K})$
growth of Section~\ref{subsec:theory}.
IETI-DP iterations grow from $14$ to $29$; as explained in
Section~\ref{subsec:theory}~(ii), the spectral bound~\eqref{eq:kappa-ieti}
predicts roughly constant conditioning (since $H/h\approx\sqrt{n_k}\approx 5$
throughout), so the cost increase is primarily algebraic---each CG step
requires $K$ local back-solves and the Lagrange multiplier system scales
with $K$.

\subsection{Three-dimensional benchmark: teapot surface}
\label{subsec:bench-3d}

The teapot is an open NURBS surface in $\R^3$ with $\npatch = 32$ patches
(Figure~\ref{fig:refcase}). The physical domain is a two-dimensional Riemannian
manifold $\mathcal{M}=\gmap(\refdom)$ embedded in $\R^3$ ($d = 2 < D = 3$), so the
geometry map has a non-square Jacobian $J_{\gmap}=D\gmap\in\R^{3\times 2}$ and
induces the \emph{first fundamental form} (metric tensor)
$G:=J_{\gmap}^{\top}J_{\gmap}\in\R^{2\times 2}$, symmetric positive definite
wherever $J_{\gmap}$ has full column rank. With $\alpha\equiv 1$, the model
problem~\eqref{eq:poisson-strong} becomes the Laplace--Beltrami equation
$-\Delta_{\mathcal M}u=f$, where
$\Delta_{\mathcal M}u=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\det G}}\,
    \widehat\nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(\sqrt{\det G}\,G^{-1}\widehat\nabla\widehat u\bigr)$
and $\widehat u=u\circ\gmap$. Its weak form uses the tangential (surface)
gradient $\nabla_{\mathcal M}u=(J_{\gmap}^{\dagger})^{\top}\widehat\nabla\widehat u
    \in T_{x}\mathcal M$, with $J_{\gmap}^{\dagger}=(J_{\gmap}^{\top}J_{\gmap})^{-1}
    J_{\gmap}^{\top}=G^{-1}J_{\gmap}^{\top}$ the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse;
equivalently, pulling back to the parameter domain,
\begin{equation}
    a(u,v)=\int_{\mathcal M}\nabla_{\mathcal M}u\cdot\nabla_{\mathcal M}v\,\mathrm{d}S
    =\int_{\refdom}\bigl(\widehat\nabla\widehat u\bigr)^{\top}\,G^{-1}\,
    \bigl(\widehat\nabla\widehat v\bigr)\,\sqrt{\det G}\;\mathrm{d}\widehat x .
    \label{eq:laplace-beltrami}
\end{equation}
We follow the isogeometric surface-PDE framework
of~\cite{dede2015isogeometric}; see also the surface finite-element analysis
of~\cite{dziuk2013finite}. The field $u = \sin(x)\cos(y)$ is no longer an exact
solution, so the $L^2$ column measures inter-method agreement, not solution
accuracy.

same geometry-induced degradation that breaks the multigrid smoother, though it
remains far milder than that of the global Krylov solvers.

\section{Neural Schur preconditioner: numerical comparison}
\label{sec:nn-comparison}

We compare $\Pneural$ with scaled Dirichlet $\Psd$ and no preconditioner using
local spectra, global PCG convergence, and solve time; action loss alone is
insufficient~\cite{wu2026dlhim}.

\subsection{Spectral and timing acceptance criteria}

For a patch on which $\Sloc{k}$ is positive definite, define
\begin{equation}
    \delta_{F,k}=\frac{\|M_{\theta,k}-\Sloc{k}\|_F}{\|\Sloc{k}\|_F},\qquad
    c_k=\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\Sloc{k}^{-1/2}M_{\theta,k}\Sloc{k}^{-1/2}\right),
    \quad
    C_k=\lambda_{\max}\!\left(\Sloc{k}^{-1/2}M_{\theta,k}\Sloc{k}^{-1/2}\right).
    \label{eq:nn-local-diagnostics}
\end{equation}
For a floating patch the same definition is made on the positive eigenspace
of $\Sloc{k}$.  If $c=\min_k c_k>0$ and $C=\max_k C_k$, congruence with the
scaled jump operators in~\eqref{eq:scaled-dirichlet} gives
\begin{equation}
    c\Psd\preceq\Pneural\preceq C\Psd,
    \qquad
    \kappa(\Pneural\Fschur)
    \leq \frac{C}{c}\,\kappa(\Psd\Fschur).
    \label{eq:nn-global-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus extremal generalized eigenvalues, not the average Frobenius error alone,
control the bound.

For $X\in\{\mathrm{none},\mathrm{sD},\mathrm{NN}\}$, total solve time
$T_{{\rm solve},X}$ contains the complete PCG iteration and excludes assembly,
factorization, model loading, cache construction, and all other setup.  Every
comparison also reports iterations.

\subsection{Reproducible protocol}

All methods share the same assembly, $\Fschur$, right-hand side, initial
vector, stopping rule, and one unmeasured warm solve.  Standard cases use 40
solves and 5,000 preconditioner applications; the reference uses 200 and
20,000.  Lanczos estimates $\widehat\kappa$, and each run stores CSV data,
convergence histories, logs, and a manifest.  Timings use one Ryzen 5 7600
thread unless stated otherwise.  The neural model replaces only the local
preconditioner action, not the common augmented solves in $\Fschur$.

\subsection{Changing degree and refinement: why the vanilla model fails}
\label{subsec:nn-pr-transfer}

On the 21-patch Yeti, $p$ denotes the local spline degree and $r$ the number
of uniform refinements.  Subscripts $c$ and $f$ denote the trained coarse
level $(p_c,r_c)=(2,2)$ and the requested fine level.  For patch $k$,
$n_{c,k},n_{f,k}$ count free local basis functions and $s_{c,k},s_{f,k}$ count
active skeleton degrees of freedom.  The ONNX capacities are 304 and 48,
whereas $(p_f,r_f)=(2,3)$ needs up to 1,120 and 96.  The vanilla model is
therefore dimensionally undefined, not merely inaccurate.

To reuse it, let $x_i^f$ and $x_j^c$ be fine and coarse physical skeleton
coordinates.  Row-normalized inverse-square interpolation from the four
nearest $x_j^c$ defines $C_k\in\R^{s_{f,k}\times s_{c,k}}$.  Set
\begin{equation}
    P_k=C_k(C_k^\top C_k)^{-1/2},\qquad
    \Pi_k=P_kP_k^\top,\qquad Q_k=I-\Pi_k,
    \label{eq:nn-projector}
\end{equation}
so $P_k^\top P_k=I$.  Then $\Pi_k$ selects coarse-visible fine modes and $Q_k$
the new modes introduced by refinement or degree elevation.

With cached coarse neural matrix $M_{c,k}$ and
$D^J_{f,k}=0.75\,\operatorname{diag}(A^f_{\Gamma\Gamma,k})$, where
$A^f_{\Gamma\Gamma,k}$ is the fine skeleton stiffness block, define
\begin{equation}
    M^{\rm proj}_{f,k}=P_kM_{c,k}P_k^\top+Q_kD^J_{f,k}Q_k.
    \label{eq:nn-projected-operator}
\end{equation}
The orthogonal terms preserve symmetry and positive definiteness without
retraining.  Replacing $D^J_{f,k}$ by the learned $\epsilon_k I$ takes 51
iterations versus 46 without preconditioning: new modes need a stiffness
scale, not positivity alone.  Table~\ref{tab:nn-pr-transfer} and
Figure~\ref{fig:nn-pr-transfer} show that projection helps throughout the
sweep but loses ground as the unresolved fine space grows.

\subsection{Projection versus training directly on the fine grid}
\label{subsec:nn-fine-retraining}

The alternative is direct fine-grid training.  The $(p,r)=(2,3)$ Yeti dataset
has 4,032 patch--probe pairs: 1,536 canonical vectors $e_j$ (coordinate vector
$j$), 1,248 independent standard-normal probes, and 1,248 Gaussian random
field (GRF) probes.  At physical skeleton coordinates, a GRF probe $g$ has
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Cov}(g_i,g_j)
    =\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|x_i-x_j\|^2}{2\ell_f^2}\right),
    \qquad \ell_f=0.08,
    \label{eq:nn-fine-grf}
\end{equation}
Every random probe is Euclidean-normalized.  The new capacities are
$(N_{\max},S_{\max},\dv)=(1120,96,96)$; one epoch is one pass over the rows.
Table~\ref{tab:nn-fine-retraining} and Figure~\ref{fig:nn-retraining} compare
this model with projection: retraining wins after 165 epochs, but scaled

\subsection{Does a Yeti dataset generalise to other geometries?}
\label{subsec:nn-geometry}

A row-wise Yeti split tests new actions on known local matrices, not new
geometries.  We therefore apply the Yeti-only model without retraining to the
planar lake, square and L-domain and to the curved teapot.  In
Table~\ref{tab:nn-geometry}, $K$ is the patch count after optional splitting;
all cases use $(p,r)=(2,2)$, coefficient $\alpha=1$, Dirichlet conditions,
corner primals and relative tolerance $10^{-8}$.

The model does not generalize reliably: it needs 59 versus 25 iterations on
the 16-patch lake and 115 versus 37 on the teapot.  Thus the 17-step Yeti
result is geometry-specific.

\subsection{Geometry-diverse data and explicit padding masks}
\label{subsec:nn-diverse-data}

\paragraph{Geometries.}
All data use $(p,r)=(2,2)$, coefficient $\alpha=1$ and homogeneous Dirichlet
conditions.  The eight configurations are the 21-patch Yeti with its physical
$x$ coordinate multiplied by $0.5$, $1$ or $2$; the lake and square after one
or two uniform patch splits, giving $K=4$ or $16$; and the native $K=32$
teapot surface.  The L-domain is never passed to the generator.  It is therefore
an excluded geometry family, whereas a scaled Yeti is only a deformation of a
known family.
